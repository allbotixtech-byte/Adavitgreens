## Section 4 - Three Value Cards

**♻ What Is Waste Management?**
Waste management is the discipline of collecting, transporting, treating and recovering material that has reached the end of its first useful life - safely, legally, and with the maximum possible value returned to the economy.

**🌍 Why Recycling Matters**
Recycling one tonne of circuit boards recovers more gold than 17 tonnes of mined ore. Recovery isn't charity for the planet - it is the cheaper, cleaner, and increasingly the only legal way to source secondary raw material.

**📈 The Business Case**
Beyond compliance, recycling reduces landfill liability, protects brand reputation, unlocks scrap value from written-off assets, and delivers the documented evidence your ESG and CSR reporting now demands.

## Service 4 - Secure Data Destruction

**Sub-headline:** Retired hardware carries live risk. We remove both.

Formatting is not erasure. Any decommissioned drive that leaves your premises with recoverable data is a breach waiting to be discovered - and under the DPDP Act, an expensive one.

**Options available**
- **Onsite destruction** - our team performs sanitisation or physical destruction at your premises, witnessed by your IT or security officer
- **Offsite destruction** - sealed, tamper-evident transport to our dedicated destruction room with client-witnessed or CCTV-recorded processing
- **Software sanitisation** - multi-pass overwriting to recognised wiping standards, with per-device logs
- **Physical destruction** - degaussing, drilling, crushing or shredding of drives and media

**Media covered**
HDDs · SSDs · Magnetic tapes · Optical media · USB drives · Memory cards · Mobile devices · Servers and NAS units

**Deliverables**
Serial-wise Certificate of Data Destruction · Video/photographic evidence on request · Chain-of-custody documentation

### What Actually Happens to Your Laptop After You Hand It Over

**Category:** E-Waste · **Read time:** 6 minutes · **By:** Advait Green Team

Most people's mental model of recycling ends at the collection van. The machine goes in, a certificate comes back, and whatever happened in between is somebody else's process.

It's worth knowing what that process is - partly because it explains why authorised recycling costs what it does, and partly because it explains why the informal alternative is so damaging.

**Stage 1: Weighment and registration**
Every consignment is weighed at entry and logged against the manifest it travelled on. Serial numbers are recorded where asset-level tracking has been requested. Nothing moves further into the facility until the paperwork reconciles with the physical count.

**Stage 2: Data-bearing media removal**
Before anything is dismantled, storage media is separated. Drives go to a dedicated destruction area and are handled under a separate chain of custody, with their own certificate. This step happens first for a reason - once a machine enters the dismantling floor, it becomes very hard to guarantee where any individual component went.

**Stage 3: Manual de-manufacturing**
A laptop is not one material. It is roughly `[X]` distinct fractions - an aluminium or polycarbonate casing, a steel chassis, a lithium-ion battery, a display assembly containing its own hazardous components, a motherboard dense with gold, palladium and copper, and a scattering of plastics with different polymer identities.

Machine shredding all of that together destroys most of the recoverable value and contaminates every output stream. So the first pass is done by hand, by trained dismantlers, to separate fractions before mechanical processing begins.

**Stage 4: Hazardous fraction segregation**
Batteries, mercury-containing backlights, capacitors and CRT glass where applicable are separated into dedicated storage. These are the fractions responsible for most of the environmental harm attributed to e-waste, and they need controlled downstream handling rather than recovery on site.

**Stage 5: Mechanical processing**
What remains - casings, chassis, cabling, mixed metals - goes through shredding, followed by magnetic separation for ferrous metal, eddy-current separation for non-ferrous, and density separation for plastics. Each output stream is graded and stored separately.

**Stage 6: Downstream channelling**
Printed circuit boards, the most valuable fraction, are consolidated and sent to authorised refiners with the metallurgical capability to recover precious metals safely. Granulated plastics go to reprocessors. Ferrous and non-ferrous metal goes to smelters.

**Stage 7: Documentation**
The consignment closes with a Certificate of Recycling, weighment records, a Certificate of Data Destruction where applicable, and the entries required for the generator's own compliance filings.

**Why the informal route is different**
The informal chain skips stages two through five. Boards are heated over open flames to release solder, cables are burned to strip insulation, and acid baths are used to leach gold - in residential areas, without protective equipment, with the residues going into soil and groundwater. It recovers a fraction of the material and imposes the rest as a cost on everyone nearby.

That gap - between what a formal facility recovers and what an informal operation destroys - is the entire argument for authorised recycling.
